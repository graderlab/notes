# Verbosity bias and self-preference: what the numbers say

Two more documented judge behaviors, both measurable and both independent of
answer quality.

## Verbosity bias

LLM judges tend to reward longer answers. In controlled comparisons where the
same content is presented in short and long forms, the long form scores higher.
The correlation between answer length and assigned score shows up even when
length carries no information.

Practical consequences:

- Rubric-only judges (no length signal) still show the effect, because the
  model's prior preference leaks through.
- Some harnesses counter it with length penalties or by asking the judge to
  ignore style — mitigation is partial at best.

## Self-preference

Judges prefer answers generated by their own model family. The effect is
smaller than position or verbosity bias but persists across templates. It is
one of the reasons judge-model choice is part of any eval's disclosed
methodology.

## What this means

These biases are properties of the judge, not of you. Documenting them is how
harness builders calibrate; knowing them is how submitters read their scores.
